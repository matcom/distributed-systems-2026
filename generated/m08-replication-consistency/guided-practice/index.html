    Replication and Consistency

    Part IIIChapter 9Guided practice

    Core practice: Complete cases 1, 3, 4, 5, 9, 11, 13, 17, 20, and 21. Then use the Practice target at the end of this page as the stop criterion. The remaining cases are a reusable retrieval/challenge bank, not a checklist you must finish before the lab.

    Complete these before the lab. Work from invocation/response histories and explicit replica state; do not classify a system from product labels such as “replicated” or “eventually consistent.”

    1. Durability versus consistencyCore

    Section titled “1. Durability versus consistency”

    A three-replica service acknowledges after writing only R1, then asynchronously copies to R2/R3.

    Immediately after OK, R1 fails permanently.

    Answer separately:

    • Was the completed write durable against one replica loss?
    • Could the system still be highly available before the failure?
    • Does eventual convergence help recover a value that existed nowhere else?

    2. Synchronous acknowledgementChallenge bank

    Section titled “2. Synchronous acknowledgement”

    Compare three policies for N=3:

    A: acknowledge after 1 durable replica
B: acknowledge after 2 durable replicas
C: acknowledge after all 3 durable replicas

    For each, describe normal write latency sensitivity and the number/type of unavailable replicas that block writes.

    3. Linearizability historyCore

    Section titled “3. Linearizability history”

    Initial x=0.

    C1: write(1) ---- OK
C2:                  read() -> 0

    Explain why this violates linearizability.

    Now move C2’s read so it overlaps the write and construct a legal linearization that allows result 0.

    4. Sequential but not linearizableCore

    Section titled “4. Sequential but not linearizable”

    Construct a two-client history that preserves each client’s operation order and can be arranged into one legal sequential register history, but violates cross-client real-time precedence required by linearizability.

    Explain the difference explicitly.

    5. Causal historyCore

    Section titled “5. Causal history”

    Alice writes A. Bob reads A and then writes B. Carol independently writes C.

    Draw the happened-before constraints.

    Which orders involving C may differ across replicas under causal consistency?

    6. Eventual consistency contractChallenge bank

    Section titled “6. Eventual consistency contract”

    A design says only:

    If writes stop and communication remains available, all replicas eventually converge.

    List five client-visible behaviors that this sentence does not specify.

    7. Read-your-writesChallenge bank

    Section titled “7. Read-your-writes”

    A client writes version 8 to R1, then is routed to lagging R2 and reads version 7.

    Propose three different ways to provide read-your-writes without necessarily making every read globally linearizable.

    8. Monotonic readsChallenge bank

    Section titled “8. Monotonic reads”

    A session observes versions:

    10, 12, 9, 14

    Identify the violation. Design a client-side or routing policy that prevents it.

    9. Quorum arithmeticCore

    Section titled “9. Quorum arithmetic”

    For each N, choose an R and W satisfying R+W>N:

    • N=3
    • N=5
    • N=7

    Then state whether 2W>N also holds and what write-quorum intersection it provides.

    10. Quorum availabilityChallenge bank

    Section titled “10. Quorum availability”

    For N=5, R=3, W=3:

    • how many unavailable replicas can a read tolerate?
    • how many can a write tolerate?
    • what happens in a 2+3 partition?

    Answer per side, not globally.

    11. Quorum is not the whole protocolCore

    Section titled “11. Quorum is not the whole protocol”

    Two replicas in a read quorum return:

    R1: value=A, version=8
R2: value=B, version=8

    Why is R+W>N insufficient to decide the result? What additional metadata or rule is needed?

    12. Primary/backup failureChallenge bank

    Section titled “12. Primary/backup failure”

    Primary P sends a write to B1 but not B2, then crashes before replying to the client.

    Construct at least two possible client retry histories depending on which node later becomes authoritative.

    Which problem belongs to Chapter 4 semantics, and which requires Chapter 7-style agreement?

    13. Partition policyCore

    Section titled “13. Partition policy”

    Three replicas split as:

    R1 R2   X   R3

    Design two valid policies:

    • one prioritizing single-copy/linearizable-style safety;
    • one prioritizing write availability on both sides with later conflict resolution.

    For each, state the user-visible cost.

    14. CAP wording auditChallenge bank

    Section titled “14. CAP wording audit”

    Critique the sentence:

    “Our database chooses consistency and availability, so it is not partition tolerant.”

    Rewrite it as an operational statement about what reads/writes do when communication partitions the replicas.

    15. Last-writer-wins and skewChallenge bank

    Section titled “15. Last-writer-wins and skew”

    Replica A writes with wall timestamp 1000. Replica B, whose clock is 30 seconds fast, concurrently writes timestamp 1030.

    A last-writer-wins merge selects B.

    What has the system learned about causality? What has it merely imposed as a deterministic policy?

    16. Read repairChallenge bank

    Section titled “16. Read repair”

    R1/R2/R3 return versions 10, 10, and 8.

    Describe a read-repair process and identify what could go wrong if version ordering itself is ambiguous.

    17. Recovery criterionCore

    Section titled “17. Recovery criterion”

    After a partition heals, define progressively stronger recovery milestones for a stale replica:

    • reachable;
    • membership healthy;
    • missing state identified;
    • state transferred;
    • state verified;
    • safe for the claimed read/write model.

    Which one should a benchmark use when claiming “replica recovery time”?

    18. History versus final stateChallenge bank

    Section titled “18. History versus final state”

    Create a history in which all replicas end at x=5 but a client observed a value that violates the claimed linearizable history.

    Explain why final convergence cannot detect the violation.

    19. Membership-change dangerChallenge bank

    Section titled “19. Membership-change danger”

    Old replica set:

    {A,B,C}

    New replica set:

    {C,D,E}

    Show why clients using different membership versions can choose quorums that do not overlap as intended. Why is safe reconfiguration a coordination problem?

    20. CRDT merge lawsCore

    Section titled “20. CRDT merge laws”

    Three replicas of a grow-only counter contain:

    A = [3,1,0]
B = [2,4,0]
C = [3,0,5]

    Merge by component-wise maximum.

    Compute:

    • merge(A,B);
    • merge(B,A);
    • merge(merge(A,B),C);
    • merge(A,merge(B,C));
    • merge(A,A).

    Use the results to explain commutativity, associativity, and idempotence.

    Then ask a harder question: which application invariants does this counter not protect?

    21. Convergence versus invariant preservationCore

    Section titled “21. Convergence versus invariant preservation”

    Two disconnected replicas each allow a withdrawal because each locally sees balance 100.

    Replica A accepts -80. Replica B accepts -70.

    After merge, both replicas agree on the same history/state.

    Explain why convergence does not prove that the global non-negative-balance invariant was preserved.

    Name one design that would require coordination and one application whose updates could safely use a merge-oriented design.

    22. AI-audited history classificationChallenge bank

    Section titled “22. AI-audited history classification”

    Ask an AI assistant to classify three operation histories as linearizable or not.

    For each answer, verify manually:

    • operation intervals;
    • real-time precedence;
    • legal single-copy order.

    Record at least one place where the model relies on timestamp/order intuition without constructing a valid linearization.

    Practice target

    Section titled “Practice target”

    You are ready for the lab when you can explain why none of these implications is valid by itself:

    replicated          => durable
replicated          => linearizable
quorum              => complete consistency protocol
final convergence   => strong intermediate consistency
