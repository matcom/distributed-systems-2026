    Reference view. This page is optional context; return to the Lesson for the required path.

    Chapter 11 connects database transactions, ownership, consensus-backed state, network failure, and horizontal scale. It also prepares the decentralized placement mechanisms of Chapter 5.

    Connection to Chapter 4 — RPC ambiguity

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 4 — RPC ambiguity”

    Transaction protocol messages are remote calls and inherit the same uncertainty:

    PREPARE sent
response lost

    or:

    COMMIT sent
acknowledgement lost

    Stable transaction IDs and idempotent protocol handlers are therefore not optional implementation polish; they are necessary retry semantics.

    Connection to Chapter 8 — naming and ownership metadata

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 8 — naming and ownership metadata”

    A sharded service must answer:

    which shard owns key K?

    That mapping is a naming/discovery problem with stronger authority semantics.

    During rebalancing, stale routing information should be detected through an ownership epoch/version rather than silently creating two writers.

    Connection to Chapter 3 — concurrency control

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 3 — concurrency control”

    2PC does not solve concurrent access to the same participant state.

    Local locks, version validation, or another isolation mechanism still protects each participant’s local invariants.

    Prepared transactions may hold those resources while waiting for a remote decision, coupling distributed failure to local contention.

    Connection to Chapter 6 — time

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 6 — time”

    Timeouts can trigger suspicion/retry but do not reveal the global transaction decision.

    Saga deadlines and lease-like reservations similarly require explicit timing assumptions.

    Connection to Chapter 10 — failures

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 10 — failures”

    Coordinator and participant crashes create different knowledge states.

    The most important distinction is:

    participant is PREPARED and decision unknown

    versus:

    participant knows ABORT

    Silence cannot safely collapse those states.

    Connection to Chapter 9 — replication

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 9 — replication”

    Each shard may itself be replicated.

    A system can therefore contain:

    replication within shard
+
partitioning across shards

    These dimensions should be analyzed independently.

    Connection to Chapter 7/Chapter 10 — consensus and state machines

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 7/Chapter 10 — consensus and state machines”

    Consensus can make each shard’s authoritative state resilient.

    2PC coordinates one transaction across several such authorities.

    Conceptually:

    Raft group A ─┐
              ├─ 2PC transaction
Raft group B ─┘

    Consensus and atomic commit are complementary rather than interchangeable.

    Connection to databases

    Section titled “Connection to databases”

    This module directly intersects:

    • atomic transactions;
    • two-phase locking;
    • two-phase commit;
    • write-ahead recovery;
    • sharding;
    • range/hash partitioning;
    • rebalancing;
    • serializability/isolation.

    You should preserve the distinction between atomic commit and isolation.

    Connection to Chapter 5 — DHTs and decentralized placement

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 5 — DHTs and decentralized placement”

    Chapter 11 treats consistent hashing mainly as a way to reduce data remapping and support partition ownership.

    Chapter 5 asks what happens when lookup/membership should no longer depend on one centralized routing map.

    That adds overlay routing, churn, stabilization, and gossip.

    Connection to Chapter 12 — performance

    Section titled “Connection to Chapter 12 — performance”

    Cross-shard work creates fan-out:

    one client operation
→ many participant operations

    The slowest required shard can dominate tail latency. Hot shards and rebalancing traffic can also create uneven resource pressure.

    Design question for every project

    Section titled “Design question for every project”

    Before introducing a distributed transaction, ask:

    Can the ownership model be changed so the invariant is local to one shard?

    If the answer is no, then ask:

    Does the problem require atomic commit, or can it tolerate an explicit multi-step workflow with compensation/eventual convergence?

    That decision is more important than the choice of transaction library.
