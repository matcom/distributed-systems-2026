    Coordination II — Mutual Exclusion, Elections, and Agreement

    Part IIChapter 7Guided practice

    Core practice: Complete cases 0a, 0b, 0c, 1, 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, and 16. Then use the Practice target at the end of this page as the stop criterion. The remaining cases are a reusable retrieval/challenge bank, not a checklist you must finish before the lab.

    Complete these before the lab. Begin by separating the coordination problem; once you reach Raft traces, annotate term, role, vote, log, and majority evidence rather than reasoning from node names alone.

    0a. Mutual exclusion comparisonCore

    Section titled “0a. Mutual exclusion comparison”

    Three processes need exclusive access to one non-replicated device.

    Compare:

    • one central coordinator;
    • permission from peers;
    • one circulating token.

    For each, state:

    • the safety rule;
    • the normal message path;
    • one crash that threatens liveness;
    • what recovery must avoid doing twice.

    Which design makes lost authority easiest to detect? Which one makes duplicated authority especially dangerous?

    0b. Bully-style electionCore

    Section titled “0b. Bully-style election”

    Processes have IDs:

    A=1 B=2 C=3 D=4

    D is the coordinator and becomes unreachable from A/B/C. B starts a Bully-style election.

    Trace who B contacts and which process should eventually announce itself coordinator if C is alive.

    Then add a partition in which D is still alive but isolated. Explain why choosing C does not prove D instantly knows it lost authority.

    0c. Election is not consensusCore

    Section titled “0c. Election is not consensus”

    Suppose C wins the election, but old coordinator D previously accepted command X that only D stored.

    Answer separately:

    1. who is coordinator now?
    2. is X committed?
    3. may C overwrite the same logical position?
    4. what extra protocol state is needed before the answer is safe?

    Your answer should make clear why leader election ≠ replicated agreement.

    1. Majority intersectionCore

    Section titled “1. Majority intersection”

    For N=5, list three different majorities of size 3. Show that every pair intersects.

    Then explain why intersection is only useful if the intersecting node preserves safety-relevant state.

    2. Split voteCore

    Section titled “2. Split vote”

    Five followers enter term 7. A and C both become candidates.

    Votes:

    A <- A,B
C <- C,D
E has not voted

    Can either candidate become leader? What events can lead to a later successful election without violating safety?

    3. One vote per termChallenge bank

    Section titled “3. One vote per term”

    Suppose node B grants a vote to A in term 4, replies, crashes before persisting votedFor, then restarts and grants a vote to C in term 4.

    Construct how this can undermine the one-leader-per-term argument. Which write-order/persistence rule fixes it?

    4. Higher-term step downChallenge bank

    Section titled “4. Higher-term step down”

    Leader L operates in term 9. It receives an AppendEntries or RequestVote message carrying term 11.

    What must L do with its role and current term? Why is continuing as a term-9 leader unsafe?

    5. Election timeout semanticsCore

    Section titled “5. Election timeout semantics”

    A follower starts an election after missing heartbeats.

    What has it learned about the old leader? Which statement is justified?

    • “The leader definitely crashed.”
    • “I have not observed valid leader communication within my election timeout.”

    Connect this to Chapter 10.

    6. Log freshnessChallenge bank

    Section titled “6. Log freshness”

    Two candidates request a vote.

    Candidate A: lastLogTerm=5, lastLogIndex=12
Candidate B: lastLogTerm=6, lastLogIndex=9

    According to Raft’s lexicographic freshness rule (last term first, then index), which log is more up to date? Why can a shorter log be newer in this sense?

    7. Candidate with same last termChallenge bank

    Section titled “7. Candidate with same last term”
    A: lastLogTerm=6, lastLogIndex=9
B: lastLogTerm=6, lastLogIndex=12

    Which is more up to date?

    8. AppendEntries prefix checkCore

    Section titled “8. AppendEntries prefix check”

    Leader sends:

    prevLogIndex=5
prevLogTerm=3

    Follower has index 5 with term 2.

    Should it append the new entries? What response should drive the leader’s next action?

    9. Conflicting suffixChallenge bank

    Section titled “9. Conflicting suffix”

    Leader:

    1:t1 2:t1 3:t2 4:t4 5:t4

    Follower:

    1:t1 2:t1 3:t2 4:t3 5:t3 6:t3

    Identify the matching prefix and the suffix that may be replaced.

    10. Local presence versus commitmentCore

    Section titled “10. Local presence versus commitment”

    Entry X exists on two of five nodes. The current leader crashes.

    Can you call X committed? What additional evidence is required?

    Now suppose X exists on three nodes. Is “three copies” alone always enough to apply X immediately under Raft? Explain the current-term commit nuance.

    11. nextIndex and matchIndexChallenge bank

    Section titled “11. nextIndex and matchIndex”

    For follower F:

    nextIndex[F]=8
matchIndex[F]=7

    What does each value mean? If F rejects because its log diverges before index 7, which value should the leader adjust first?

    12. Minority old leaderCore

    Section titled “12. Minority old leader”

    Five nodes partition:

    A B   X   C D E

    A was leader before the partition.

    Explain:

    • why A may still believe it is leader;
    • why A cannot commit a new entry with only B;
    • how C/D/E can elect a newer-term leader;
    • what happens to A when the partition heals.

    13. Uncommitted old-leader entryChallenge bank

    Section titled “13. Uncommitted old-leader entry”

    A old leader appends command X to itself and B in a 2-of-5 minority partition.

    The 3-node majority elects D and commits command Y at the same index.

    After healing, what should happen to X? Why is this not loss of committed data?

    14. Commit-index exerciseChallenge bank

    Section titled “14. Commit-index exercise”

    Five-node leader has match indexes:

    Leader: 12
B:      12
C:      11
D:      8
E:      7

    Which highest index is replicated on a majority? What additional term condition must be checked before the leader advances commit directly under Raft?

    15. commitIndex versus lastAppliedChallenge bank

    Section titled “15. commitIndex versus lastApplied”

    A follower has:

    commitIndex=20
lastApplied=17

    What should it do next? Is the consensus log inconsistent merely because application state is temporarily three entries behind?

    16. Client timeout after commitmentCore

    Section titled “16. Client timeout after commitment”

    Client sends request R7. Leader commits it, crashes before replying, client retries to the new leader.

    What does consensus guarantee? What does it not guarantee for the client-visible side effect?

    Which Chapter 4 mechanism must reappear in Chapter 10?

    17. Safety versus liveness timingChallenge bank

    Section titled “17. Safety versus liveness timing”

    Network delay grows without bound.

    Which Raft properties should still hold? Which progress properties may stop?

    Explain why this separation matters.

    18. Paxos comparisonChallenge bank

    Section titled “18. Paxos comparison”

    For each concept, write the closest conceptual role without claiming exact equivalence:

    • Raft term;
    • Paxos proposal/ballot number;
    • Raft majority;
    • Paxos acceptor quorum;
    • stable leader in Multi-Paxos;
    • Raft leader.

    Then state one reason line-by-line translation is misleading.

    19. App voting versus consensusChallenge bank

    Section titled “19. App voting versus consensus”

    Three AI agents vote 2–1 on answer X.

    List at least five properties missing before this can be called a fault-tolerant distributed consensus/replicated-log mechanism.

    20. AI-audited Raft traceChallenge bank

    Section titled “20. AI-audited Raft trace”

    Give an AI assistant a five-node election/log trace and ask which entries are committed.

    Audit the answer manually. Check whether the model confuses:

    • local presence;
    • majority replication;
    • current-term commitment;
    • application.

    Record one invariant-based correction if needed.
